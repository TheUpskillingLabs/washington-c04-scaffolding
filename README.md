# Scaffolding

**How much structure does a learner need, when, and from whom?**

A Research Pod of The Upskilling Labs of Greater Washington, formed at sensemaking day, Oct 24, 2026. We work through frame creation, Kees Dorst's method (*Frame Innovation*, 2015): we find out why the problem is hard before we build anything.

## Where we started

On sensemaking day we worked from intuition. We went looking for the paradox and came away with hunches. Here's how the pod first put it:

From the October 3 retreat, tension 01: **guidance vs. autonomy.** It drew more survey responses than any other.

As the pod first wrote it:

- Structure, framework and syllabus **vs.** free-form "learn by doing" with guides
- Peer-to-peer support and discovery **vs.** top-down instruction

**Who seems to be involved:** Upskillers · mentors · workshop leaders · facilitators

We own what happens in the room. How progress is seen from outside is [Wayfinding](https://github.com/TheUpskillingLabs/washington-c04-wayfinding). We read each other's repos every week.

## Why we build on OLOS

The Upskilling Labs of Greater Washington is building OLOS, the Labs' open-source platform, for itself. The region runs its own instance and changes it to fit how the region works. What holds up goes back to core OLOS as pull requests, so the next region starts from what Washington learned. OLOS is the idea and the open-source project. The Upskilling App at theupskillinglabs.org is the national core's instance of it, less than a year old. Ours is the region's own. Research The Labs, not the app. Build on OLOS, not around the app's limits.

## The nine steps, and when

| Step | What it asks | When |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Archaeology | How did this become a problem, who has tried to solve it, anywhere, and what happened? What does everyone assume? | Now to Nov 7 |
| 2. Paradox | Why is it hard? Which two things are both true and pull against each other? | Nov 7 |
| 3. Context | Who is closest to it at The Labs, and what does each need? | Now to Nov 7 |
| 4. Field | Who else, anywhere, faces something like this? What do they do? | Now to Nov 28 |
| 5. Themes | What shows up across all of them? | Nov 7 |
| 6. Frames | A new way of seeing the problem that makes the paradox easier to move | Nov 8 to 14 |
| 7. Futures | What does the frame make possible for each player? Show them. Do they want it? | Nov 14 |
| 8. Transformation | Change how The Labs works, built on OLOS, released weekly to real people | Nov 15 to Dec 5 |
| 9. Integration | The change becomes part of how the region runs, someone keeps it, and it goes upstream | Dec 8 |

Budget 4 to 6 hours of your own time a week, not counting your coding agent. If a week needs more, cut scope.

## Archaeology: where to look

Not just our attempts. Anyone, anywhere, who has tried to solve this.

- Apprenticeships, Cooperative Extension, bootcamps that closed, self-directed programs. How did they decide how much structure to give, and when to take it away?
- Our own pilot and Cycles C01 to C03: what structure each used, what changed, why. Where "get stuck first" came from.
- Who raised tension 01 at the Oct 3 retreat, in what words, and what they wanted fixed.
- The survey responses on guidance vs. autonomy. Count them, quote them.

Talk to one person, or find one attempt. Open an issue for each thing you find: what it is, where it came from, when, and what it tells us. Say when something is hearsay. **No names, recordings or transcripts in this repo.** Keep raw notes yourself.

## Projects

After Nov 14, each builder in residence leads one project and recruits a team. A project is a change to how The Labs works that a real person uses, built on the region's OLOS instance and sent upstream as a pull request. Code lives in the region's OLOS fork on a branch named `washington-c04-scaffolding-projectname`, no hyphens inside the project name (for example, `washington-c04-scaffolding-mentorbench`). This repo gets a branch of the same name holding the project's README. `main` holds the pod's research.

## Maintainers

Three to five builders in residence. Each leads one project.
